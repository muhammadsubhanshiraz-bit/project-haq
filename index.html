§ Preamble

What is
Project HAQ?

Across Pakistan, millions of people living with disabilities face barriers that go far beyond the physical — barriers in law, in education, in employment, and in how institutions choose to, or fail to, implement the protections that already exist on paper.

Project HAQ exists to close that gap. We look at how schools, NGOs, and institutions in Karachi actually measure up against the legal protections owed to persons with disabilities — auditing real compliance, not just paper policy.

Law isn't just theory. It's a tool — and a tool only works when people understand it, use it, and hold institutions accountable to it.

The question we keep asking

Are the rights written into law actually being felt by the people they're meant to protect?

§ Why the Law Matters

Five reasons legal protection isn't optional

Without law, inclusion is left to charity, goodwill, or chance — and that has never been enough.

Cl. 1

Recognition

Law formally recognizes persons with disabilities as full citizens with equal rights — not objects of pity or charity.

Cl. 2

Access

Law mandates physical accessibility, educational accommodation, and workplace adjustment — turning "should" into "must."

Cl. 3

Employment

Quota systems require employers to reserve jobs for persons with disabilities, countering systemic exclusion from the workforce.

Cl. 4

Non-discrimination

Anti-discrimination law gives people an enforceable remedy — not just a moral complaint.

Cl. 5

Accountability

Law creates obligations for the state. Without it, protection is voluntary. With it, it's required.

§ Pakistan's Legal Record

Where the country stands

Pakistan ratified the UN Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities in 2011. Since the 18th Amendment devolved legislative power, each province has taken its own path.

2011

CRPD Ratified

Pakistan formally commits to the UN Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities — a major statement of intent.

2014

KP Amendment 2% quota

Khyber Pakhtunkhwa updates its old ordinance, but still has no comprehensive disability rights act of its own.

2017

Balochistan Act 5% quota

Mandates a 5% employment quota and reserves seats in educational institutions.

2018

Sindh Act 5% quota

Widely cited as the most progressive provincial law — raises the quota from 2% to 5%, with inclusive education and fee concessions.

2020

ICT Act, Islamabad

Adopts a modern, CRPD-aligned definition covering physical, sensory, intellectual, and psychosocial disability.

2022

Punjab Act 3% quota

Enforces a 3% employment quota across public and private organizations.

Legislation is only the first step. Quotas often go unmet, "reasonable accommodation" is inconsistently defined, and enforcement varies sharply by province. This is exactly the gap Project HAQ exists to explore — not just whether the laws exist, but whether they're actually being lived.
